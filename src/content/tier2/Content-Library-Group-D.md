# Content Library — Group D: Ranked Gap Descriptions

**21 pieces (7 pillars × 3 severity levels) · Blocks Sprint 9 · Target: Week 9**

## What these are and where they appear

The Tier 2 report contains a **ranked gap analysis** — a short, ordered list showing the founder which gaps cost them most. It sits above the full pillar-by-pillar diagnosis and gives the reader the answer before the detail.

Each line is **one sentence** naming what the gap costs in practice.

## How ranking works — and why the copy has to carry it

Gaps are ranked by **weight × (100 − pillar score)**, not by lowest score.

Worked example from the build spec: a business scores 50.00 on Partner Profile Definition (8% weight) and 65.00 on Unit Economics (20% weight). Partner Profile is the *lowest score*, but it ranks **sixth**; Unit Economics ranks **first**, because 20% × 35 = 7.0 outweighs 8% × 50 = 4.0.

A founder reading this will notice their lowest score is not at the top. **The copy has to make that feel deliberate rather than broken.** Two things do that work: an explanatory line above the list (Group E), and gap sentences that describe consequence rather than deficiency — so a reader understands they are ordered by what it costs, not by how low the number is.

## Severity thresholds

Severity is set by the pillar's own score, independent of its rank position:

| Severity | Pillar score |
|---|---|
| Major | 0–50 |
| Moderate | 51–70 |
| Minor | 71–85 |

Above 85, no gap line is shown for that pillar.

# 1. UNIT ECONOMICS (20%)

| Severity | Gap description |
|---|---|
| **Major** | The numbers do not currently leave room for both a franchisee's return and the brand's fee — which is the first thing an investor tests and the hardest objection to answer. |
| **Moderate** | Your economics work for an outlet you own, but have not been modelled for one that pays you a fee and a royalty — so the offer's real return is not yet known. |
| **Minor** | The economics hold, though payback sits at the longer end of what comparable formats offer, which will come up when an investor compares opportunities. |

# 2. OPERATIONAL REPEATABILITY (20%)

| Severity | Gap description |
|---|---|
| **Major** | Too much of how this business runs exists as knowledge rather than as process — and a franchisee cannot absorb what your team knows by working alongside them. |
| **Moderate** | Routine operations are documented but judgement-dependent decisions are not, which is where consistency breaks first across a network. |
| **Minor** | Documentation is substantially complete, with gaps in specific areas that each franchisee would otherwise resolve their own way. |

---

# 3. SUPPORT INFRASTRUCTURE (15%)

| Severity | Gap description |
|---|---|
| **Major** | There is no structured capacity to support a partner after signing — which is what the franchise fee is nominally paid for, and what an investor will ask about directly. |
| **Moderate** | Support currently depends on your personal availability, which caps the network at whatever your time allows and reaches that limit sooner than expected. |
| **Minor** | Support exists but its scope is not stated, so a franchisee's expectations would be formed during the sale rather than defined in the offer. |

---

# 4. FOUNDER & LEADERSHIP READINESS (15%)

| Severity | Gap description |
|---|---|
| **Major** | The business still runs through you, and franchise support is an additional demand on time that is already the constraint. |
| **Moderate** | Operations are delegated but decisions are not, so partner support would compete directly with the daily load you already carry. |
| **Minor** | The business runs substantially without you, though some retained decisions are operational rather than strategic and will multiply across a network. |

---

# 5. MARKET READINESS (12%)

| Severity | Gap description |
|---|---|
| **Major** | Demand has only been proven where the brand is already known, so a franchisee elsewhere would be testing an assumption rather than buying a proven model. |
| **Moderate** | The model has worked in more than one place, but without defined site criteria you cannot assess whether a partner's proposed location will perform. |
| **Minor** | Market evidence is sound, though without an expansion sequence the network will form around where investors are rather than where the brand should be. |

---

# 6. BRAND POSITIONING STRENGTH (10%)

| Severity | Gap description |
|---|---|
| **Major** | There is little external evidence of brand strength, so an investor has no way to verify that licensing your name is worth what it costs. |
| **Moderate** | The brand has real local standing that is not visible to anyone who has not experienced it, which means it will be discounted during due diligence. |
| **Minor** | Brand presence is established but not distinct enough to be the reason a partner chooses you over a comparable opportunity. |

---

# 7. PARTNER PROFILE DEFINITION (8%)

| Severity | Gap description |
|---|---|
| **Major** | You have no defined criteria for who the right partner is — and partner selection is the one franchise decision that cannot be reversed afterwards. |
| **Moderate** | You know roughly who you are looking for, but without a structured assessment, selection would turn on enthusiasm and available capital. |
| **Minor** | Criteria exist but are not applied consistently, which is difficult to invoke once a first exception has been made. |

## Notes for the Team

**One sentence, no more.** These sit in a scannable list. A founder reads the ranked gaps to get the headline; the diagnosis blocks in Group C carry the detail. If a gap line runs to two sentences, the material belongs in Group C instead.

**Name the consequence, not the deficiency.** "Your SOPs are incomplete" states a fact the score already conveyed. "A franchisee cannot absorb what your team knows by working alongside them" states what the fact costs. The second is why the reader keeps reading.

**Same boundary as Group C — these diagnose, they do not prescribe.** No gap line should contain a verb the founder is meant to act on. "Without defined site criteria you cannot assess a partner's location" is diagnosis. "Define your site criteria" is Tier 3's roadmap.

**Consistency with Group C is deliberate.** Each gap line is the compressed version of its corresponding diagnosis block. When editing Group C, check whether the matching gap line still holds — if a diagnosis is rewritten around a different consequence, the one-liner should follow.

**The claims worth checking are the same ones flagged in Group C.** The Partner Profile major line asserts that partner selection cannot be reversed; the Support Infrastructure moderate line asserts the capacity limit arrives sooner than founders expect. Both are load-bearing and both should be confirmed against real engagements before they ship.
