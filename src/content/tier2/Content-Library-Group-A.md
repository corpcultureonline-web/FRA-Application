## A1. FL01 — Anchored Descriptor (5 anchors)

**Question shown to the founder:**
> How much of the day-to-day operation currently runs without you?

*Note: the question is deliberately phrased around what happens, not how comfortable the founder feels. Comfort is a feeling and founders exploring franchising are optimistic by selection. What the business actually does is observable.*

| Score | Anchor text |
|---|---|
| **1** | I am involved in most daily decisions. If I am away for a week, things slow down or wait for me. |
| **2** | My team handles routine tasks, but decisions — purchasing, staffing, customer issues — still come to me. |
| **3** | A manager runs the day-to-day within limits I have set. I am consulted on anything outside those limits. |
| **4** | Managers make operational decisions independently. I review outcomes weekly or monthly rather than approving in advance. |
| **5** | The business operates without my involvement in daily decisions. My role is direction and growth, not operations. |

**Why this works:** each anchor describes an observable arrangement. Two founders selecting "3" mean the same thing, which is what makes the score comparable across businesses and usable for calibration.

**[CHECK]** — is the 1–5 spread right for the businesses you actually see? If most Indian SME founders in your client base cluster at 1–2, the scale may need recalibrating so it discriminates where your respondents actually sit rather than compressing them all at the bottom.

## A4. Verdict Paragraphs (5 pieces)

Shown at Tier 1 beneath the range. Each must work alongside a **straddled band statement**, since a 16–17 point range crossing a band boundary is the normal case, not the exception.

### Band 1 — Not Yet Franchisable (0–40)

> Your responses place you in the early part of the range, and the honest read is that franchising now would be premature. That is not a judgement on the business — plenty of good businesses are not yet franchisable, and several of the gaps here are fixable within a year. What matters is not attempting to franchise around them. Ten questions can only see so far; the Readiness Report examines forty-four and will show you specifically which gaps are structural and which are simply undone.

### Band 2 — Early Stage (41–60)

> You have the beginnings of something franchisable, but the foundations are not yet load-bearing. Businesses in this range usually have a working model and real customers — what they lack is the documentation, systems or support capacity that lets someone else reproduce the result. The distance from here to franchise-ready is measured in months of deliberate work, not years. The Readiness Report will identify which of the seven pillars is actually holding you back, and how far behind it sits.

### Band 3 — Emerging — Franchisable with Conditions (61–75)

> You are close. Businesses in this range are generally franchisable, but with specific conditions that need resolving before partners are brought in — and the cost of skipping them is usually paid by the first franchisee. Your preliminary result points to where the constraint lies, but ten questions cannot tell you how deep it runs. The Readiness Report resolves your exact position across all seven pillars and tells you which conditions are genuinely non-negotiable.

### Band 4 — Franchisable — Ready to Structure (76–90)

> Your foundations are sound. Businesses in this range are ready to move from preparing to structuring — which means the questions that matter next are about model, fee, territory and partner selection rather than whether to franchise at all. That is a good position to be in, and a small number of things at this stage still commonly go wrong. The Readiness Report shows your exact standing on each pillar and flags anything that should be settled before you take the opportunity to market.

### Band 5 — Highly Franchisable — Scale-Ready (91–100)

> This is a strong preliminary result. Businesses scoring in this range typically have proven economics, documented systems and the leadership capacity to support partners — the conditions under which franchising works as intended. The risk at this stage is not readiness but pace: expanding faster than the support structure can carry. The Readiness Report confirms your position pillar by pillar and identifies where scale would strain first.

**Note on tone:** none of these congratulate or console. Each names the position, states what it means, and points to Tier 2 as the resolution of a genuine ambiguity rather than as an upsell.

## For the Team

**The pattern to follow when filling these in:**

Every piece names something specific and consequential. Nothing says "this needs improvement" — it says what the gap costs and who pays for it. That is the difference between a report a founder forwards to their co-founder and one they close after skimming.

Three tests before any piece is accepted:

1. **Would a founder recognise their own business in this**, or could it have been written about anyone?
2. **Does it stay inside the tier boundary** — diagnosis only at Tier 1 and 2, never a recommended action?
3. **Is every claim true** — particularly claims about cost, timeline, or what "usually" happens? Generic advice is forgettable; a wrong specific is worse.
